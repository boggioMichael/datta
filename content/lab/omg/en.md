## The game

A see-through bubble of life floats in a soft dawn. A stem feeds it from a seed pod, and out of the stem falls a crawling baby. Every run starts there, with nothing but babies. Two identical cells that touch fuse into the next one up, and the new cell is bigger and bounces out: walking, running, a bicycle, a donkey, a pony, a horse, an elephant, a Vespa, a Harley, a Kia Picanto, a Mini, a Jeep, a Porsche, a Ferrari F40, a Bugatti Chiron, a fire engine, an excavator, the Bagger 288, the Mallard, a Shinkansen, a trireme, a pirate galleon, a clipper, a superyacht, an aircraft carrier, a 747, Concorde, the SR-71, the Saturn V, the Space Shuttle, Starship — then mechs, saucers, warp drives and a time machine — then a space elevator, an O'Neill cylinder, a ringworld and a Dyson sphere — then the rides of the gods: Hermes' sandals, Pegasus, Sleipnir, Thor's goats, Garuda, Ra's barque, Helios' chariot — then Moses' basket, Noah's Ark, Jonah's great fish, the parting of the Red Sea, the Ark of the Covenant, Jacob's ladder, Elijah's chariot, Ezekiel's wheels within wheels, the cherub, the Messiah's white donkey, and finally the last boss: the God of Israel.

That is 155 tiers in 19 eras, and sizes are real: every tier is much wider than the one below, so Hermes' sandals are vastly bigger than the Space Shuttle. The world can't hold that, so it keeps pulling back. Every era has its own place around the bubble — a meadow, a town at golden hour, a highway at dusk, a city at night, a desert, misty mountains, the open sea, the deep, the clouds, orbit, a nebula, the galaxy, the realm of the gods, the heavens — and the land shrinks away as the camera pulls back, dust and lights stream inward, and out of focus behind the bubble float the giants the pile is about to become. When the babies are too small to see, they clump in the stem into one cell, and the climb goes on.

Every merge shouts “oh my god”, deeper and louder at each step. You can pour more babies, poke the bubble's wall, stir its fluid with a finger, tilt gravity, pinch to zoom, and use six tools. When God is born every other light goes out, the bubble strains around the light and bursts — and God tells how it all began, reading Genesis 1:1–2:3 in Hebrew or English. At each moment of creation the story waits for you: touch the darkness to let there be light, swipe up to lift the sky and to raise the dry land, touch the sky to light the sun, touch the water, touch the earth, press and hold to breathe life into the first people, and light the Sabbath candles. In between, every touch plants a tree, hangs a star, or sets a fish, a bird or a beast into the world.

## Options

Eight modes (Classic, Watch, Chaos, Mega, Trampoline, Black Hole, Time Attack, Sandbox), six tools (shake, magnet, bomb, slow-motion, gravity flip, upgrade), a manual camera, and 37 settings: speed up to ×8, pour rate up to a flood, gravity from the Moon to Jupiter, bounciness, vessel size, growth per tier, journey length, random events, chain combos, a bounce melody, voice styles, drama level, cell styles, the living world, depth and motes, the old neon rings for nostalgia, flashes, labels, trails, phone tilt and an editable reel-style caption. A collection keeps every tier you have reached, with its sound.

## How it is built

The whole game is a Rust crate with no dependencies, compiled to WebAssembly. It owns the physics, the rules, the ladder, the camera and its zoom, the living world of every era, the bubble, the interactive creation story and its timeline, the options and the progression, and it produces a list of draw commands every frame; a thin JavaScript shell paints that list on a canvas, forwards touches and plays the sound.

The sound is Rust too. A second instance of the same WebAssembly runs in a Web Worker and renders every sound on demand: it takes one of 33 short voice clips (generated once with the open Kokoro speech model) and pushes it through pitch-shifting, time-stretching, vocoder choirs, reverb and a bed of synthesised effects, then masters it louder at every tier. The Genesis soundtrack was mixed by the same engine: a Kokoro voice made deeper and wider for God, over a chord for every day, wind over the deep, waves, harps, choirs, bells and a held breath while creation waits for you. The Hebrew is the Masoretic text; the English is the 1917 JPS translation.

The 168 illustrations were drawn for the game as hand-written SVG. Model names appear for the joke; there are no company logos.
