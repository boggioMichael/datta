## The game

A see-through bubble of life floats in a soft dawn. A stem feeds it from a seed pod, and at the stem's tip hangs the next cell — at first, a crawling baby. You aim it and decide when it falls, and two identical cells that touch fuse into the next one up: walking, running, a bicycle, a donkey, a pony, a horse, an elephant, a Vespa, a Harley, a Kia Picanto, a Mini, a Jeep, a Porsche, a Ferrari F40, a Bugatti Chiron, a fire engine, an excavator, the Bagger 288, the Mallard, a Shinkansen, a trireme, a pirate galleon, a clipper, a superyacht, an aircraft carrier, a 747, Concorde, the SR-71, the Saturn V, the Space Shuttle, Starship — then mechs, saucers, warp drives and a time machine — then a space elevator, an O'Neill cylinder, a ringworld and a Dyson sphere — then the rides of the gods: Hermes' sandals, Pegasus, Sleipnir, Thor's goats, Garuda, Ra's barque, Helios' chariot — then Moses' basket, Noah's Ark, Jonah's great fish, the parting of the Red Sea, the Ark of the Covenant, Jacob's ladder, Elijah's chariot, Ezekiel's wheels within wheels, the cherub, the Messiah's white donkey, and finally the last boss: the God of Israel.

That is 155 tiers in 19 eras, and sizes are real: every tier is wider than the one below, so Hermes' sandals are vastly bigger than the Space Shuttle. The world can't hold that, so it keeps pulling back. Every era has its own place around the bubble — a meadow, a town at golden hour, a highway at dusk, a city at night, a desert, misty mountains, the open sea, the deep, the clouds, orbit, a nebula, the galaxy, the realm of the gods, the heavens — and the land shrinks away as the camera pulls back, dust and lights stream inward, and out of focus behind the bubble float the giants the pile is about to become. When the babies are too small to see, they clump in the stem into one cell, and the climb goes on.

Like evolution, every new era is a moment of change: the camera pulls back, the world turns into the next one, and the road of evolution appears — every era from the baby to God, with the light running along it to the new one. Every new tier is born inside a double helix of light that winds around it, and at the end your own evolution is waiting: when you reached each era.

Every merge shouts “oh my god”, deeper and louder at each step. When God is born every other light goes out, the bubble strains around the light and bursts — and God tells how it all began, reading Genesis 1:1–2:3 in Hebrew or English. At each moment of creation the story waits for you: touch the darkness to let there be light, swipe up to lift the sky and to raise the dry land, touch the sky to light the sun, touch the water, touch the earth, press and hold to breathe life into the first people, and light the Sabbath candles. In between, every touch plants a tree, hangs a star, or sets a fish, a bird or a beast into the world.

## The journey

The journey is a game of skill, and it takes hours. Inside the stem you can see the next two cells, and a dotted line shows where the one in hand will fall — even when wind or tilted gravity carries it aside. The bubble has a strain line: a pile that stays above it for a few seconds bursts the bubble, and you go back to the start of the era. Every burst in the same era makes the bubble tougher — the line rises a little, it waits longer, and every ability has at least one charge — so no era is a wall. Every era is saved, and the journey saves itself all the time, so you can close it and carry on later.

Every era brings a law of nature to understand: a nursery where you can't burst yet, the strain line, mixed litters of bigger cells, rush hour when a cell you hesitate over drops on its own, a roomy era, speed, the slam, scrap that only a fusion touching it can smash, monster litters, the sway of a railway car, a crosswind that changes direction, tides that raise and lower the strain line, thin air, gravity that turns, antimatter that annihilates small cells, gravity wells, divine cells that lift a tier, miracles without warning, and at the end, the narrow way. Six abilities unlock along the way — shake, slow-motion, magnet, bomb, gravity flip and upgrade — and chain reactions charge them.

Four difficulties: Relaxed (about two hours to God), Normal (four to five hours for a good player), Hard, and Epic — a marathon where every tier takes twice the cells. In the engine's tests a bot plays the whole journey at every level, to tune the difficulty and to make sure God can be reached.

## Options

Eight modes (the Journey, Watch, Chaos, Mega, Trampoline, Black Hole, Time Attack, Sandbox) — in every mode but the Journey the stem pours by itself, and you can poke the bubble's wall, stir its fluid with a finger and tilt gravity — six tools (shake, magnet, bomb, slow-motion, gravity flip, upgrade), a manual camera, and 40 settings: the journey's difficulty, a fast start from several points, reel length, speed up to ×8, pour rate up to a flood, gravity from the Moon to Jupiter, bounciness, vessel size, growth per tier, run length, random events, chain combos, a bounce melody, voice styles, drama level, cell styles, the living world, depth and motes, the old neon rings for nostalgia, flashes, labels, trails, phone tilt and an editable reel-style caption. A collection keeps every tier you have reached, with its sound.

## How it is built

The whole game is a Rust crate with no dependencies, compiled to WebAssembly. It owns the physics, the rules, the ladder, the journey and every era's law of nature, the saves, the camera and its zoom, the living world of every era, the bubble, the interactive creation story and its timeline, the options and the progression, and it produces a list of draw commands every frame; a thin JavaScript shell paints that list on a canvas, forwards touches and plays the sound.

The sound is Rust too. A second instance of the same WebAssembly runs in a Web Worker and renders every sound on demand: it takes one of 33 short voice clips (generated once with the open Kokoro speech model) and pushes it through pitch-shifting, time-stretching, vocoder choirs, reverb and a bed of synthesised effects, then masters it louder at every tier. The Genesis soundtrack was mixed by the same engine: a Kokoro voice made deeper and wider for God, over a chord for every day, wind over the deep, waves, harps, choirs, bells and a held breath while creation waits for you. The Hebrew is the Masoretic text; the English is the 1917 JPS translation.

The 168 illustrations were drawn for the game as hand-written SVG. Model names appear for the joke; there are no company logos.
