## The film

*A Limit* is not a film about discovering hidden truths. It is a film about the moment a person realizes that the process by which they determine truth may itself be unreliable. The central conflict is not between reality and illusion; it is between two competing models of reality.

Matheo is not a philosopher. He does not search for abstract truth. He searches for stability. Every decision he makes is an attempt to preserve a coherent model of the world, and as that model repeatedly fails, the story becomes less about solving a mystery and more about what kind of person he will become.

## How it is written

From the filmmaker's document that governs every decision on the project:

> The audience should not merely understand uncertainty. They should experience it. Not through confusion. Not through missing information. But through the gradual collapse of interpretations they once believed were unquestionably true. Every major revelation redefines something the audience has already witnessed.

The audience always knows where they are, what Matheo wants, and what decision he is facing. Only one thing remains uncertain: *can Matheo continue to trust the way he experiences reality?* Nothing exists only because it is strange, and nothing exists only because it is symbolic; every unusual event must first work as believable human experience. The film never argues for a worldview. Different viewers should reach different conclusions while remaining equally supported by what they saw.

When two creative decisions compete, one question decides: *which deepens the audience's emotional relationship with Matheo?*

## The audiobook

The screenplay is being released first as an audiobook — read as a screenplay, scene headings and all, rather than rewritten into prose. The format is part of the experience: the listener is handed the same instructions a crew would receive, and watches the film in their head. A first full narration was recorded in September 2026; editing follows.

## The model underneath

The screenplay grew out of a philosophical model about what a conscious subject can represent but never contain — death, other minds, the limit of its own standpoint. That model is written up separately, labelled honestly as a speculative proposal, in [[research/the-limit-of-experience]]. The film does not argue the model; it dramatizes the experience the model describes.
