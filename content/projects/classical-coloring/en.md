## The idea

A fugue is a visual object as much as an audible one: a subject that enters, is answered, inverts, stretches, piles up. On the page, all of that is black. Colour Scores would print the score with a structured colour map — one colour per voice, a second system for motifs, a third for cadences — and leave the colouring itself to the reader. A child fills in the shapes; a student learns where the subject hides; a listener follows a recording with a pencil.

## Copyright, plainly

Two different rights apply to a piece of sheet music, and the project respects both:

- **The composition.** Works by composers who died more than seventy years ago are in the public domain almost everywhere. Bach, Mozart, Chopin: free to use.
- **The edition.** A modern engraving of a public-domain composition is itself a protected work — the editor's fingerings, layout and corrections belong to the publisher. Colour Scores would use editions that are themselves public domain, or engrave the music fresh.

Nothing under copyright is used without a licence. This is the kind of distinction that gets skipped and should not be.

## Status

A concept with notes. No prototype yet. If a first volume happens, it will be one short piece, printed in a small run, and judged by whether anyone colours it.
