Definitions, rules, and first principles are rarely the most difficult elements of a story to communicate. Tradition merely insists they be concealed.

Most authors therefore elect to introduce them indirectly. They construct characters, furnish them with ambitions, fears, habits, and carefully selected imperfections, then allow the architecture of their universe to emerge as an accidental by-product of conflict. Ideally, the reader arrives at the final page convinced he has experienced a story, never realizing he has merely been instructed.
