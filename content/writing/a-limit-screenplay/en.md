## EXT. CITY STREET — NIGHT

Matheo gets out of a bar. Tipsy. The environment is desolate and eerily quiet, illuminated by a moon that erases all but the deepest shadows. The atmospheric tone is disorienting and heavy with internal conflict.

### Scene 1

== A LIMIT ==

As he left the bar, he came to a crossroads of decisions.

Leaving his car behind, he began to walk. The moon almost erased the shadows. Almost.

As the gay voices faded, it encased him; it was the silence of animals. Fireflies drifted through the night air, searching for the light on their own backs. As if the whole world sighed “Fin.”

## EXT. RESIDENTIAL STREET — NIGHT

Matheo walks alone under the night sky. The setting is intimate yet lonely, focused on his phone and the raw nature of the street life around him. The tone is contemplative and somber.

The wine settled warmly inside him; heat grew inside him. It made him think of her.

He opened his phone. He pressed her number, still bloody scarlet, flushed from the hunt. He opened her profile photo. She remained silent as the night. Waiting.

MATHEO (wondering): For me?

Wandering in his own mind. His thumbs hovered over the glass.

Walking for a couple of beats, hypnotized by the fear of a wrong word. He passed a pair of grey cats mating in the street, their entanglement raw and exposed. He watched for a moment, then looked away. Letting his impulse fade in the cool air.

The cats remained locked together, oblivious to everything except themselves. He shoved his phone into his pocket.

## EXT. PARK — NIGHT

Matheo approaches a bridge within a park of gentle hills and ominous trees. The environment is dark and atmospheric, defined by the contrast of yellow lamps and tarry water. The tone is suspenseful and shifting.

The park rolled into gentle hills beneath the moon. High, ominous trees stood along each side of the beautiful twisted brick road. There was a bridge. The water beneath was agitated, tarry, and dark.

On the bridge lay yellow lamps. Black water, and white shine. He decided to cross.

The lights of the town almost shattered across the tarn. Almost. Making the scene almost enlightened. Almost.

He walked slowly; rain lingered in the smell of the leaves—a soft touch.

He walked slowly, not because he was tired, but because he had silence.

Seven lanterns divided the bridge into seven precise pieces. Their warm yellow-reddish light cut the darkness into seven equal pools.

He walks past one. Behind the growl of an engine. Second. It crescendos. He doesn’t look behind. The engine grew louder. Even more. More.

A last smell of rubber.

Silence.

MATHEO (thinking): Where’s the car?

He would have staked his soul on its presence at his back. Matheo pressed onward. Silent. Disturbed.

A beat. He looked down. The brick road was beautiful, with a geometric precision that felt wrong. He looked up. The light from the lantern grew. He looked to the side. The tarn lay black. He looked forward. The street light grew. Green.

He frowned.

MATHEO (thinking): Hadn’t it been yellow?

The only sound was the smack of his steps. The light grew; no lantern came. Closer?

A beat. It had to be yellow.

Step. The light got greener. Step. Bigger.

Is the lantern approaching him? Does he get closer to it? He reached with his hand, expecting a cold metal touch. The hand stayed fixed in the air—as if he didn’t want it to touch the light. Why didn’t he want to?

The light grew.

A faint childlike melody drifted through the silence—the simple jingle repeated. The fanfare swelled. Bright. Triumphant. Familiar. Again.

The light grew. More urgent, louder. The melody folded into itself. Again. Again. No beginning. No end. One.

### Scene 2 — The First Paradox

## INT. MATHEO’S CHILDHOOD BEDROOM — LATE AFTERNOON

Eight-year-old Matheo is in his cluttered sanctuary, joined later by his Father. The room is a hazy, golden environment filled with childhood relics and the hum of a CRT screen. The atmospheric tone is nostalgic yet becomes increasingly heavy and oppressive with the news of death.

The room is messy in the effortless way only a child’s room can be. Plastic dinosaurs. LEGO castles. A soccer ball beneath the desk. Sunlight spills through the curtains. A CRT television hums softly.

On the screen—SUPER MARIO WORLD. The bright ending fanfare plays as Mario reaches the goal.

Eight-year-old MATHEO grins. Without taking his eyes off the television, he perfectly times another jump.

The bedroom door suddenly opens. Matheo startles. Mario jumps with him. He laughs.

His FATHER stands in the doorway. Still wearing his work clothes and holding his car keys. He smiles—but only with his mouth.

FATHER: Doing well?

Matheo shrugs.

MATHEO: I’m almost done.

His father nods. He doesn’t leave. Instead, he quietly walks into the room and sits on the edge of the bed. The game continues. Mario runs through another level. Only the music fills the room. Matheo notices.

MATHEO: ...Dad?

His father looks at the floor. Then at his son. He tries once. Stops. Tries again.

FATHER: ...Your grandpa...

A long pause.

FATHER: ...Grandpa died today.

Matheo keeps looking at the screen. Mario jumps over a Koopa.

MATHEO: ...Okay.

His father waits. Nothing.

FATHER: You understand? He died.

Another pause.

MATHEO: When are we going to Grandma’s?

His father smiles sadly.

FATHER: We will.

Silence. Matheo reaches for the controller again. His father gently places a hand over it.

FATHER: No... I mean... You won’t ever see Grandpa again.

Matheo finally turns toward him.

MATHEO: ...Why?

His father searches for words. Because. Gone. He can’t say any of them.

FATHER: ...Because when people die... they don’t come back.

Matheo thinks, only for a second.

MATHEO: Where did he go?

His father opens his mouth. Nothing comes out.

FATHER: I don’t know.

Matheo blinks.

MATHEO: You don’t know?

FATHER: No.

Silence. The game keeps playing. Matheo looks back at the television.

MATHEO: ...Will you die?

His father closes his eyes.

FATHER: ...Yes.

MATHEO: My mom?

FATHER: ...Yes.

MATHEO: My sister?

FATHER: ...Yes.

Another silence.

MATHEO: ...Me?

His father nods.

FATHER: ...One day.

Matheo stares at the screen. He presses RIGHT. Mario keeps running. He lets go. Mario keeps running. He presses LEFT. Nothing. Jump. Nothing. Every button. Nothing. Mario continues moving to the right. Steadily. Without stopping.

Mario keeps running.

Matheo frowns.

MATHEO: ...Dad.

His father leans forward.

FATHER: What?

MATHEO: The controller...

He takes it. Presses every button. Nothing. Mario keeps running. Neither of them speaks.

Ahead—the castle. Mario reaches it. The familiar ending theme begins. Bright. Cheerful. Victorious.

The screen should fade. It doesn’t. Mario remains standing in front of the castle. The ending theme starts again. The same. Then again. Again. With every repetition—slightly faster. Again. Faster. Again.

The notes become unnaturally sharp. Too cheerful. Too perfect. The music begins to sound less like celebration—and more like panic. Mario never disappears. The screen never fades. He simply stands there. Trapped. The music loops. Again. Again. Again.

Matheo’s breathing changes.

MATHEO: ...Dad.

His father walks to the television.

FATHER: It must’ve frozen.

He presses RESET. Nothing. He turns the console off. The music continues. He unplugs the console. The music continues.

He stares. Confused now.

FATHER: ...That’s...

He reaches behind the television. Pulls the power cable. The screen collapses into a white line. Then—black.

For a fraction of a second—the glass becomes a mirror. His father stands behind Matheo. But something is wrong, only for an instant. His father’s face seems to carry Matheo’s eyes. His mouth. His expression. Blink—it’s gone.

The room is silent. The music has stopped. His father forces a laugh.

FATHER: Old televisions.

He plugs everything back in. The Nintendo logo appears. Everything works perfectly as if nothing happened.

His father rubs Matheo’s shoulder.

FATHER: You’ll be late if you don’t get ready.

He leaves. Matheo remains sitting on the floor. The television hums quietly. Mario smiles from the title screen. Matheo stares at him.

MATHEO: ...Where do people go?

The game cannot answer.

*CUT TO:*

## EXT. AMUSEMENT PARK — EVENING

Matheo and his parents move through the flashing neon and cool twilight of an amusement park. The background is a chaotic mix of machinery, machinery scents, and distorted shadows. The tone is jarring and disorienting as Matheo retreats into the isolation of a mirror maze.

A small family roller coaster. Plastic ghosts. Painted skeletons. Fake cobwebs. Matheo watches the entrance with excitement. The ride begins. It is slow. Gentle. Not frightening at all. Thirty seconds later—it’s over.

His parents laugh.

PARENT: See? Not so scary.

Matheo doesn’t laugh.

MATHEO: ...That wasn’t scary.

He leaves before they can answer, toward a sign that reads MIRROR MAZE. He disappears inside. Reflections multiply instantly. One Matheo. Five. Twenty. A hundred.

He laughs at first. Then turns left. A dead end. Right. Glass. Left again. More reflections. He stops. Every direction looks identical.

MATHEO: ...Mom?

Nothing.

MATHEO: ...Dad?

Only his own voice returns. Softer. Further away. For the first time that day—Matheo feels afraid.

He turns in circles. Every Matheo turns with him. None of them knows the way out.

*CUT TO BLACK.*

### Scene — “The Portrait”

## INT. ELENA’S APARTMENT — EVENING

Warm, amber light pools around an easel in a room crowded with greenery and the weight of lived-in history. The quiet scratch of a record needle fills the gaps between breaths.

Matheo and Elena are in a crowded, amber-lit apartment filled with books and plants. The environment is warm and domestic, though every object feels pointedly intentional. The atmospheric tone is intimate, intellectual, and increasingly surreal.

Golden light spills across an old apartment. Books. Plants. A record spins quietly. Elena paints. Matheo watches from the couch.

She never paints landscapes. Only faces. Today—she is painting him.

ELENA: Don’t move.

MATHEO: I’m not.

ELENA: You are.

MATHEO: I’m breathing.

She smiles.

ELENA: So am I.

Silence. Only the brush against canvas. Matheo studies the room. Every object feels strangely... intentional. Every cup. Every book. Every scratch in the wooden table. Almost too real.

ELENA: What are you thinking?

He hesitates.

MATHEO: ...Have we met before?

She laughs.

ELENA: I hope so.

MATHEO: No.

He frowns.

MATHEO: I mean before... before.

She looks at him.

ELENA: You ask strange questions.

MATHEO: I keep feeling...

He searches for the words.

MATHEO: ...that you’re remembering me.

ELENA: I am.

MATHEO: No.

He shakes his head.

MATHEO: I mean before we met.

Her smile fades. She continues painting.

ELENA: You always want there to be another answer.

MATHEO: What if there is?

ELENA: What if there isn’t?

Silence. Matheo stands. Walks toward the unfinished portrait. She quickly turns the canvas away.

ELENA: Not yet.

MATHEO: Why?

ELENA: Because it isn’t finished.

He reaches anyway. She gently catches his wrist.

ELENA: Please.

He stops. Looks into her eyes. For a second—their breathing becomes synchronized, exactly synchronized. He notices. She doesn’t.

MATHEO: ...Elena.

ELENA: Hm?

MATHEO: Can you prove you’re not me?

Silence. She laughs—a real laugh.

ELENA: You really know how to flirt.

MATHEO: I’m serious.

ELENA: So am I.

She lets go of his wrist. Walks into the kitchen.

ELENA: I’m making tea.

Matheo can’t let it go. He follows her.

MATHEO: No, listen.

ELENA: I’m listening.

MATHEO: No—really listen.

She fills the kettle.

MATHEO: What if everyone is... the same person?

She looks up. Her expression changes. Not emotionally. Physically. Her jaw. Slightly broader. Her posture. His posture. Her smile. His smile. Barely noticeable. The audience could almost believe they imagined it. Matheo freezes.

MATHEO: ...Elena?

ELENA: What?

Her voice is lower now. Just enough to disturb. He steps backward.

MATHEO: No...

She steps toward him.

ELENA: Matheo.

Another step.

ELENA: What are you afraid of?

MATHEO: You.

ELENA: Or yourself?

He stares. Her face is becoming familiar. Too familiar. The kettle begins to whistle. Louder. Louder.

Matheo closes his eyes.

MATHEO: No... I don’t want you to be me.

The whistle stops. Silence. He opens his eyes. Elena is herself again. Completely. She looks confused.

ELENA: What happened?

He breathes. Nothing. Then—he smiles—a tired smile.

MATHEO: I’m sorry.

ELENA: For what?

MATHEO: For trying to solve you.

She studies him.

ELENA: You know...

She walks over. Places the paintbrush in his hand.

ELENA: I don’t think people are puzzles.

He looks at the unfinished portrait. The face on the canvas... isn’t his. It’s hers. He hadn’t noticed before. She stands behind him.

ELENA: Finish it.

MATHEO: I don’t know how.

ELENA: You don’t have to make it look like me.

She gently guides his hand.

ELENA: You only have to stop making it look like you.

For the first time, he paints without trying to correct the image. Without comparing. Without measuring. The portrait slowly becomes unmistakably Elena. Outside, the wind rises. No music. Only silence. A peaceful silence. For the first time in the film, silence does not feel empty.

### Scene 1 — Hospital

## INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — NIGHT

Sterile white light vibrates against pale walls, creating a clinical cage. The rhythmic, mechanical beep of a monitor punctuates the heavy, filtered silence.

Matheo lies in a bed surrounded by his Father, Mother, a Nurse, and a Doctor. The environment is a sterile, fluorescent clinical cage defined by mechanical beeps. The tone is clinical, paranoid, and frighteningly personal.

Darkness. A slow, mechanical beep. Then—fluorescent white. Matheo opens his eyes. The ceiling swims in and out of focus. He tries to lift his hand. Nothing. Whispers. Someone crying. His vision sharpens.

His FATHER stands beside the bed. His MOTHER. A NURSE. A DOCTOR. They’re all staring at him. Normal. Almost. His father’s smile. It is his own. The nurse scratches the side of her nose. Exactly the way Matheo does when he’s anxious. The doctor raises one eyebrow. His eyebrow.

Matheo blinks. Everything returns to normal. His father is simply his father again.

DOCTOR: Can you hear me?

Matheo nods.

DOCTOR: Do you remember what happened?

A long silence.

MATHEO: ...A car.

DOCTOR: What car?

The bridge. The green light. The engine. The melody. His mouth opens. Nothing comes out.

The doctor glances at his father.

DOCTOR: Memory loss is common.

Matheo whispers—

MATHEO: Did you... see it?

DOCTOR: See what?

They all lean closer. For one impossible instant—every face becomes Matheo’s. Different ages. Different clothes. The same eyes. The same mouth. The same fear.

Matheo jerks upright. The heart monitor screams.

MATHEO: No.

The nurse reaches for him. Her voice. His voice. Higher. Softer.

NURSE: Please lie back.

He pushes her away. The doctor restrains him.

DOCTOR: You’re safe.

Matheo stares into the doctor’s eyes. They are his own.

MATHEO: You... aren’t real.

Nobody reacts. As though he never spoke. His father steps forward. Places a warm hand on his forehead.

FATHER: Matheo.

Matheo begins to cry.

MATHEO: I don’t want to be alone.

His father smiles. This time—it isn’t Matheo’s smile. It belongs to someone else. For the first time since waking, Matheo relaxes—the monitor steadies.

Then—far away—the ending theme from Super Mario World. Soft. Almost hidden beneath the fluorescent hum. Nobody notices. Only Matheo.

He slowly turns toward the dark television screen. In its reflection—everyone standing around his bed is Matheo. He spins back. They are normal again. Silence. The melody stops as if it had never existed.

### Scene 2 — The Lake

## EXT. LAKE — SUNSET

The horizon bleeds violet and orange over a mirror-still expanse of water. A gentle breeze carries the earthy dampness of the shore as the world settles into a deepening hush.

Matheo and Elena sit by the shore as the sun sets. The horizon is a mirror-still expanse of violet and orange water. The atmospheric tone is peaceful, philosophical, and tinged with the possibility of connection or finality.

Wind moves gently across the water. Matheo sits beside ELENA. Neither of them speaks. She skips a stone. Three jumps. Four. Gone.

ELENA: I used to think that if you stare at the water long enough... you’ll eventually see yourself.

Matheo looks into the lake—only sky.

MATHEO: No. You only see what you’re afraid of.

She laughs.

ELENA: You always make everything philosophical.

MATHEO: I don’t mean to.

A comfortable silence. She leans against him. For the first time, he notices a tiny scar beneath her left eyebrow. He has never seen it before.

MATHEO: Where did you get that?

ELENA: What?

MATHEO: The scar.

She touches her forehead.

ELENA: I’ve always had it.

He frowns. Impossible. He would have remembered.

ELENA: You don’t actually look at people, do you?

MATHEO: What?

ELENA: You look through them.

*“You look through them.”*

Her words sting. She stands. Walks toward the edge of the lake. The setting sun catches her hair. For a moment, she is unmistakably herself. Different. Unreachable. Beautiful not because she is perfect—but because she is not him.

Matheo watches. A thought enters his mind: *What if she’s only another version of me?* She stops walking without turning around. Her shoulders shift. Subtly. Almost like his own posture. Her voice changes. Barely.

ELENA: Matheo?

She turns. For the briefest fraction of a second—his own face looks back at him. He gasps. She blinks. The illusion disappears. Only Elena remains. Concerned.

ELENA: What happened?

MATHEO: Nothing.

She walks back toward him. Takes his hand. He hesitates. Then squeezes it. The reflection in the lake changes. Two silhouettes. Not one. The wind carries the faintest hint of a familiar melody. It almost begins. Almost. Then dies away. Matheo closes his eyes. Just for a moment, he believes she might truly exist.
